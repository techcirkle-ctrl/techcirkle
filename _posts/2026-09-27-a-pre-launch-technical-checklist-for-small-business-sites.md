Everything on this list is cheap before launch and awkward afterwards. I run it as a gate rather than a wish list, in this order.

## Forms and their downstream effects

Start here, because it is the item most often broken and least often checked.

- Submit every form on the production domain, from a real device, and confirm the record arrives at every downstream destination — email, CRM, spreadsheet, whatever the client actually reads.
- Confirm the confirmation. The visitor must see unambiguous success or an actionable error, never a silent redirect.
- Capture attribution at submission time: referrer, campaign parameters, landing page. This is unrecoverable later and it is the only way the owner will ever know what the site is worth.
- Add a synthetic check that submits the real form on a schedule and alerts on failure or on a missing downstream record.

That last item is the highest-value monitoring available for this class of site. Silently broken contact forms routinely run for months, and the business experiences it as a slow market rather than as a fault.

## Structured data, validated

- One connected `@graph` rather than scattered blocks. A `LocalBusiness` subtype node, `Service` nodes referencing it as `provider` via `@id`, and `FAQPage` on pages with visible question-and-answer content.
- `openingHoursSpecification` including genuine holiday exceptions, `areaServed`, `telephone`, and `sameAs` for profiles the business controls.
- Pricing on services where the client will publish it. Most omitted field, most asked question.
- Validate with a structured-data testing tool and confirm the markup matches visible content exactly. Markup contradicting the page is a durable liability.

## Performance, measured properly

Measure on a mid-range mobile device over a throttled connection, not on the build machine.

- Images in modern formats, correctly sized, with explicit `width` and `height` to prevent layout shift.
- No render-blocking third-party scripts. Analytics and tag managers deferred; chat widgets loaded on interaction rather than on page load.
- `font-display: swap`, one preloaded face, aggressive subsetting.
- Map and video embeds behind a facade that loads the real payload on click.
- Set the target as a number in the contract and make final payment contingent on it. That clause does more than any conversation.

## Accessibility, tested rather than asserted

- Keyboard traversal of every interactive path, with visible focus states throughout.
- Form controls with programmatically associated labels and error messages.
- Contrast checked against the stated standard, including hover and disabled states.
- Real text rather than text rendered into images, and captions on video.
- No overlay widget. They are widely criticised, frequently interfere with the assistive technology they claim to assist, and have themselves attracted complaints.

## Content model

- Services modelled as structured entities — name, plain-language summary, price or range, duration, service area, question-answer pairs — with the page, the structured data and any booking flow derived from that one definition. This makes markup and visible content structurally unable to diverge.
- Each FAQ heading phrased as a customer would ask it, with the answer in the first one or two sentences and self-contained enough to stand alone.
- No near-duplicate pages generated by templating a variable such as a town name. One page per location genuinely served, with real local specifics.

## Integrations

- Every outbound third-party call queued rather than inline, with retry, exponential backoff and a monitored dead-letter destination.
- Idempotency keys and a processed-event record on every webhook handler. Delivery is at-least-once; without this a retried payment webhook double-credits and the error surfaces in a bank reconciliation weeks later.
- A scheduled reconciliation job comparing local state to each provider's state, alerting on divergence. All integrations drift.
- Timezone handling verified across a daylight saving transition for anything involving scheduling. This defect appears twice a year with total reliability.

## Observability

- Structured logging with a request identifier that survives across service boundaries.
- Error tracking with alerts routed to a human who will act.
- Uptime monitoring on the domain and on any booking or payment endpoint separately, since the homepage can be fine while checkout is not.

## Ownership and handover

- Domain, DNS, hosting, analytics, error tracking, email delivery and payment provider all in the client's name with the client's billing.
- Repository under the client's organisation.
- A handover document another developer could work from: local setup, deployment procedure, environment variables, third-party accounts, and known limitations.
- An editing path the client's own team can use without training. If a price change requires a support ticket, the content will go stale and eventually mislead customers.

## Post-launch first fortnight

- Verify analytics is recording and attribution is populated with real traffic, not just test traffic.
- Re-submit every form once real DNS and email routing are live.
- Check index coverage and confirm no staging URLs or noindex directives survived the cutover.
- Read the error tracker daily for two weeks. The interesting failures appear once real users arrive with real inputs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the most commonly missed pre-launch check?

Submitting every form on the production domain and confirming the record reaches every downstream destination, plus a scheduled synthetic submission afterwards. Silently broken forms run for months and are read as a quiet market.

### Why validate that structured data matches visible content?

Because markup contradicting the rendered page is a durable liability. Deriving both from one structured content definition makes divergence impossible rather than merely discouraged.

### Where should performance be measured?

On a mid-range mobile device over a throttled connection, never on the build machine. Set the target as a contractual number with final payment contingent on it.

### Why do webhook handlers need idempotency keys?

Because delivery is at-least-once. A retried payment webhook without an idempotency key and processed-event record double-credits an account, and the error surfaces in reconciliation weeks later.

### What belongs in a handover document?

Local setup, deployment procedure, environment variables, third-party accounts and known limitations — enough for a different developer to work from without contacting the original team.
